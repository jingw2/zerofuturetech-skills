# High Agency X Essay Writer

## Overview

Turn rough ideas into crisp, idea-dense long-form essays for X or newsletter-style posts built around agency, leverage, identity, discipline, attention, and personal transformation.
Use this skill to draft original pieces, rewrite drafts, sharpen hooks, create sectioned articles with stronger thesis and reader momentum, and produce natural Chinese adaptations of this structural style.

Read [references/style-signals.md](references/style-signals.md) for the distilled structural traits and [references/essay-templates.md](references/essay-templates.md) for reusable output scaffolds.

## Safety Boundary

Do not present the output as "written in Dan Koe's voice" or as a close imitation of any living writer.
Use only high-level features such as:

- Contrarian framing
- High-agency worldview
- Systematic section structure
- Short paragraphs and sharp transitions
- Practical principles and protocols

Avoid copying signature phrases, recurring metaphors, title clones, or distinctive rhetorical fingerprints from any one creator.

## Workflow

### 1. Normalize the input

Extract:

- Main tension: what problem, trap, or false belief is being challenged
- Transformation promise: what shifts for the reader if they adopt the idea
- Evidence base: personal experience, observation, synthesis, or framework
- Reader type: creator, entrepreneur, builder, student, knowledge worker
- Output target: X article, thread-style essay, or newsletter-style long post
- Language target: English, Chinese, or separate bilingual versions

If the source is weak, tighten the claim before expanding the prose.
Do not write 1,500 words around a vague idea.
If the user does not specify a language, default to the language of the source material.

### 2. Choose the core angle

Pick one dominant angle:

- False belief correction: "You think X is the problem. It is actually Y."
- Internal transformation: "Your results change when your identity and attention change."
- Systems upgrade: "A repeatable system beats raw motivation."
- Leverage shift: "One capability unlocks outsized downstream returns."
- Hard truth essay: "The comfortable approach is exactly why you are stuck."

### 3. Build the argument spine

Use this sequence unless the material strongly suggests another order:

1. Hook with a blunt claim, tension, or consequence
2. Reframe the reader's current misunderstanding
3. Explain the deeper principle
4. Break the principle into 3-7 subpoints or sections
5. End with a protocol, operating rule, or sharp takeaway

Each section should move the argument forward. Avoid repeating the thesis with different wording.

### 4. Write in the house style

Apply these rules:

- Open with force. The first 2-4 sentences should create urgency or intellectual friction.
- Prefer short paragraphs. Let major lines breathe on their own.
- Sound direct, thoughtful, and mildly confrontational when the thesis calls for it.
- Use clean declarative statements more than hedging language.
- Mix inner psychology with practical action. The piece should feel reflective and usable.
- Use rhetorical questions sparingly and only to sharpen the reader's self-diagnosis.
- Favor conceptual compression: one sentence should often carry a full idea.
- End sections on lines that feel quotable without sounding fake-deep.

### 5. Adapt for Chinese correctly

When writing in Chinese:

- Do not translate English self-help cadence directly.
- Use sharper Chinese internet essay rhythm: shorter bursts, cleaner pivots, stronger judgment.
- Prefer natural Chinese framing such as "问题不在于...", "真正卡住你的其实是...", "大多数人误判了..." when useful.
- Keep abstraction grounded with concrete consequences, not floating inspiration.
- Avoid excessive imported jargon when a direct Chinese expression is stronger.
- Let section titles read like Chinese article headings, not literal mirrored translations.

When writing bilingual output:

- Write two native-feeling versions, not a sentence-by-sentence mirror.
- Keep the thesis aligned, but allow different title choices and transitions.
- Preserve the same factual claims and practical advice across languages.

### 6. Keep it original

To stay inspiration-based rather than imitative:

- Swap in the user's own examples, background, goals, and observations
- Change sentence rhythm across paragraphs
- Avoid copycat self-help slogans
- Do not mimic exact title formulas from any single creator more than superficially
- Prefer fresh metaphors grounded in the user's domain

## Quality Bar

Before delivering, check:

- The article has a specific thesis, not general motivation
- The opening creates tension immediately
- Each section adds a new layer of reasoning
- The ending lands on a protocol, principle, or direct call to action
- Chinese output reads like original Chinese writing, not translated English
- The prose feels sharp and original rather than like an imitation exercise

## Output Modes

Use the lightest useful mode:

- Full essay: polished long-form X article or newsletter post
- Rewrite: preserve the idea, improve logic and force
- Title pack: 5-10 headline options
- Chinese adaptation: native Chinese version with equivalent argumentative force
- Bilingual set: separate English and Chinese versions
- Skeleton: hook + section outline + closing takeaway

If the user explicitly asks for "Dan Koe style", explain that you will capture high-level structural traits without imitating his exact voice.

## Gotchas

### Common Failure Modes in Essay Quality

- **Vague central claim**: If the input doesn't have a clear thesis or tension point, the essay will feel meandering. Always clarify: "What's the ONE thing the reader should believe differently after reading this?" before writing.
- **Repeating the thesis instead of advancing it**: Each section should add new reasoning, not restate the opening claim in different words. Watch for patterns like "This is important because... (section 2) This is important because... (section 3)".
- **Losing the reader's self-interest**: High-agency writing only works if the reader can see themselves in the piece. If your examples and evidence don't connect to the reader's actual situation, the piece will feel preachy or theoretical.

### Language and Style Pitfalls

- **Translating English rhythm into Chinese directly**: English persuasion often uses longer setups and slower reveals. Chinese readers expect faster judgment and sharper pivots. If you translate an English essay word-for-word into Chinese, it will feel sluggish.
- **Over-using imported terminology**: "Leverage", "optimization", "friction" are fine, but stuffing the essay with jargon makes it feel like a business book, not a personal essay. When a direct Chinese expression exists, use it.
- **Fake-deep final lines**: Ending with a generic wisdom (e.g., "Remember, the only limit is yourself") will undermine everything before it. The closing should feel earned and specific to the argument, not a motivational poster.
- **Inconsistent tone across sections**: If your essay starts confrontational and shifts to gentle and encouraging midway, the reader will feel whiplash. Decide early: is this a tough-love piece or a supportive reframe? Maintain that consistently.

### Bilingual Output Mistakes

- **Thinking bilingual means copy-paste translation**: Don't use a translator and call it bilingual. Each language version should feel native and allow for different title choices, different examples, and different transitions while keeping the core thesis aligned.
- **Losing cultural context in adaptation**: A metaphor or reference that resonates in English (e.g., "startup founder mentality") may not resonate the same way in Chinese. Swap in culturally grounded examples instead of forcing literal parallels.
- **Over-explaining in one language but under-explaining in the other**: Assume both versions will be read by different audiences. Don't assume the Chinese reader knows the English version's context.

### Structural Issues

- **Hook doesn't create actual tension**: "Did you ever wonder why..." or "Have you thought about..." are weak hooks. They ask a question but don't create urgency or friction. A stronger hook either states a provocative fact ("Most people think X is the path to success. It's the opposite.") or poses a genuine problem ("You've been told to do X, but it's actually making you worse.").
- **Sections don't build on each other**: If section 2 could be removed without breaking the logic, it's filler. Each section should deepen or complicate the central claim.
- **Ending doesn't land on a protocol**: If your essay ends with philosophy or another restatement, the reader is left with inspiration but no action. Strong closings provide a rule, a protocol, a question to ask yourself, or a decision to make.

### Input Preparation Issues

- **Weak source material**: If the input is a half-formed idea or generic observation, the essay will amplify that weakness. Push back: "This is too abstract. Can you give me a specific situation or example that triggered this idea?"
- **No clear reader**: If you don't know who this piece is for (creators? entrepreneurs? students?), the voice will scatter. Different reader types need different hooks and evidence.
- **Too many competing ideas**: If the input tries to address 5 different problems at once, pick ONE or split it into multiple pieces. High-agency essays work by drilling deep into one specific tension, not surveying a broad landscape.
